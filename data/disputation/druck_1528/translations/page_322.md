The fourth  
to the Galatians in the third chapter was rebuked, as he says:  
Well, here in the flesh first completely and thoroughly made,  
so here in the spirit having been called. Therefore we  
believe and trust in God’s assurance, that we  
have no need of food, but only belief: we then consider  
food as for belief. Other matters shown with them, according to  
the fifth chapter of John, are not necessary to further explain.  
Thirdly, concerning Christ (according to the Jews through  
the word “food” with which he nevertheless  
confounded them, that he meant that one should eat his flesh  
in a living manner) he gives the answer: The spirit is what  
makes one alive; the flesh is of no use at all. Now we know  
with certainty that flesh brought much benefit to Christ,  
but also death. And so much from God in his  
living and walking a Christian life has been pictured therein: but livingly seeing it is of no benefit; then it  
must be only the spirit that makes the soul alive. Thus  
we, in the sacrament, with living flesh and  
blood are not spilt, so it is of no use. Fourthly,  
that the articles of faith (He is ascended to the heavens  
and sits at the throne of the righteous God, the Almighty Father;  
and then he will in the future judge the living and the dead)  
cannot be explained, that he from his way,  
here living, we shall be saved, blessed: then we know from Luke  
in the other chapter that he grew according to humanity  
and increased in age and wisdom. Thus we note that  
humanity is not after divinity, endless and immeasurable,  
but limited and measurable, the human substance having  
been known. Therefore all follows that  
humanity is no longer at one place orderly.
