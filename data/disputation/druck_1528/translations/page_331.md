Schlußred. CLVIII  

Now the Scripture holds us / that our Lord has gone to heaven / we understand that he has taken humanity away from us / into the invisible glory of God / in which he will remain / for the justice of the Father / that is in the highest power / until the last judgment / then he will first appear bodily: above the conclusion I now declare / as the order of God therefore holds / that Christ Jesus, according to his appearance, should not be bodily among us / that he would be even far less likely to be given bodily in the bread. The time that he is bodily among us / that he is acted upon by us / is already far past: through his spirit and power he will make us blessed / he will be with us until the end of the world / and with this argument it is proved that Christ cannot be bodily in the sacrament / since he has gone to heaven. That he, however, was not bodily in the bread of the sacrament at the Lord’s Supper / the disciples have seen enough / he remains bodily and visible sitting with them: thus he did not raise his body to two / that one would have been poured into the bread and the other would have handed the bread over: thus the truth of the human body could not be endured / that at one time he would have been a human body and also the bread / to this the present objection shows as if our brothers held that we ate something different in the Lord’s Supper / than the disciples ate.
