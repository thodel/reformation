**Schlußred. XLV**  

Wo aber herr Prouincial die Schlußreden zewiderfechten/ willens wære/ sye jm erloubt/ gegen den predicanten/ mit heyliger geschrifft zedisputieren/ vnd on wyter vmbschweyssen frymlich zehandeln.  

*But where Lord Prouincial wishes to refute the closing speeches, he allows them to be disputed against the preacher with holy Scripture, and to act freely without further delay.*

Hienach redten die Presidenten/ als hernach volget.  

*In this respect the Prouincial protested (as if) and thereby indicated that he explains his attached writings with the following annexes, as necessary for the understanding of the matter. And so he does not give them a full place to speak, which could be allotted to serve the matter, as in a free disputation, whereby the Butzer should be left alone if he does not wish to dispute: then the Acts might be brought into order and thus they are not permitted to be dissolved as needed; it should be respected that he himself does not yet understand, so a decree was made that the mouth should be shut. Afterwards the presidents spoke, as follows thereafter.*

*My lord the president’s opinion is that, as my lord Prouincial, Doctor Wolff Capito, and Martinus Buzer, preachers of Strasbourg, have been speaking against one another for a long time, having drawn up arguments here and answered, they should no longer be allowed before the community of Bern in a proper disputation to be completed and extended. Then we, of our lord of Bern, are appointed by the presidents over each article and its way concerning any fraternal action and dispute, so that one may have a grievance against another. Therefore we devote ourselves to serving that very office alone. But with my lord Prouincial we permit free discussion and disputation with the preachers of Bern concerning each proposed article, according to the divine word, in order and according to the wishes of my noble lord of Bern, that no church or community, nor any person here present or elsewhere, may be touched, whether one believes or does not believe. Moreover, we may also freely consider that he may further explain the matter of the trade and the dispute he has with the preachers of Strasbourg, and at other times may be understood through writing or orally.*
