Word, as many people are given from God: then the external words, as elements, are not further explained; then the inner words are added, so previously they are in the heart of man, and where the external words are drawn, then such meaning and inner [sense] may be regarded as a superstition. Then what is superstition otherwise? It is that one imagines with the power of words to teach, yet the word itself cannot be taught; thus says the wise man. The words of the wise (Ecclesiastical, in the last Chapter) are all stimuli or nails. It is the human nature of the first created half, in the image, as he is created against God, firmly darkened and hardened. So that he slowly hears the true doctrine of Christ, and the glass of divine truth is not quickly taken up: by the movement and hindrance of the flesh, the true man is given some help, namely the external words, through which man, as through stimuli and nails, becomes inward, and thus learns to recognize his inner master and receives the truth. There must therefore be something recognized in man, that he may be emptied: if one for a thousand years lifts an unknown word, then one becomes nothing of this emptier; he then also understands the word. And when the words are understood, the man is inwardly moved, that he may recognize that about which it is spoken: therefore the words have been ordained, and they are not given any special power to teach further than what is said. But the spirit of God, who is hidden, gives such recognition. It would be wise that we give more to the external word, then the person who speaks such external word.
