The fourth life is a true human life. Therefore, according to the nature of human life, it can be present only in one place. Its life is, as they say, “Caro überbi,” a flesh taken from the word, yet nevertheless a true flesh; and upon this our hope rests, that also our flesh, in immortality and blessedness, may become like his flesh, because we—  

our true nature having been taken up and brought to solitary holiness—has, over many natural things, worked here on earth; but not that he would have left his life a false human life.  

The measure, however, is not wholly present at once. It is a property of the divine nature. The equalness introduced does not yet rhyme. It is far apart from one another, that one eye at once may receive many shapes and a word may be received from many ears: then that a true natural life should have its own great size and measure, being at many places, wherein we thus acknowledge that our Lord had a true natural human life; yet now, clarified, we melt his earth utterly useless, except that we make it great—namely that we are so good and gracious toward us, that he has our brother in us in all things (the sin alone having been taken) become equal, because we through a child of God have become.  

Pfarrer von Sant Gallen.
