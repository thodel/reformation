Spoke well of yesterday’s day by the Bern city clerk concerning the affairs of the lords, who are sorrowful with flies and anxiety, and are troubled. Then, where such things are not to be dismissed (for they are gladly taken from the world), they are to be discussed, and the surrounding judges and solemn judges are to be summoned with good hope, that nothing else may be turned aside, and that the business of this praised disputation and the thoughts of the modest gentlemen of Bern, on the dates, orders and decrees (as they compare themselves with Scripture) may be laid out according to the law. If so, may the gentlemen, the preachers, after their instruction, first give the closing word in the Lord’s name, take other matters, and those who support Scripture, that the gentlemen, either spiritually or worldly, may have the occasion to dispute and argue against them.

Weyster Frantzen Kolben, preacher, speaking at Bern,  

Now and peace from God the Father and our Lord Jesus Christ,  
Amen.  

You outward men and brothers in Christ,  

Since it is in all the world, in many varied burdens, that God is greatly angry against us, He has especially blessed us, for which we cannot be sufficiently thankful, in that He now moves all the world to ask after the truth and to concern themselves with judgment.  

Therefore, on all sides, not especially by the influence of the holy spirit, our prudent, wise and gracious elders, counsel and citizens of this praised place Bern, find themselves moved, you, Erbern, highly learned Christian gentlemen.
