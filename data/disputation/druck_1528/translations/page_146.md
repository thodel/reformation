Master Niclaus says that the man will be called the chief of the wives,  
therefore that he may be the higher one,  
if he does not already make them perpetual;  
thus all the higher ones of the church should be called chiefs. Answer:  
We would gladly concede to anyone that he brings with him the writing,  
that a man should rightly and properly have the Christian commonwealth,  
as a man to his **Levyb** (levy), that he may take such a chief.  
But the highest apostles, as Peter and Paul together with the wandering world,  
are ours, and we are not, however, Christ’s, in the first Epistle to the Corinthians,  
chapter three. Their are the churches, as they themselves are rummaging,  
and not **Ehegemach** (marriage); the church would otherwise not be pious,  
so they would be more than a **Gemach** (household), and therefore it remains,  
that the church has only one chief, Christ, as also only one **Gemach**,  
but the same Christ, the spiritual and bodily, who nevertheless helps according to his power,  
as every pious **Ehegemach** helps his wife according to his ability.  
Thus it is not yet proven that anyone besides Christ may be either **Ehegemach** or chief,  
to be taken as a higher one. The name “Higher” half is also sufficiently spoken of,  
in ii. 12. vff. 58 the servants are not higher, they are all alike. God be praised, that he his
