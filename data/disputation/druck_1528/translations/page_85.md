If one does not hear, then take one or two to yourself; and if he does not wish to hear the same, so says the Church. Moreover, Martin Bucer concludes that this is a single Church and not a common Council, or anything of that sort: then, if possible, all things may be expected, so that one in his successors may be without a common Council, so that something could last for a hundred years.  

But I say that such a saying should be understood as coming from a Church and from a general Council. Yet it is not entirely without note that wherever one announces his successors, it is ordained, and even if the very same neither in nor anyone with anyone wishes to hear, therefore a common Council is assembled and held.  

Yet in small matters the faith is not hastily renewed, or otherwise of another Church, one is further needed. But in the higher parts of the faith, where the Christian unity is half‑strength broken between two and no single Church wishes to hear, the common Christian Church is needed in such matters, lest the unity of the Christian Church be divided and separated in many sects and many houses, as we now sadly see before us.
