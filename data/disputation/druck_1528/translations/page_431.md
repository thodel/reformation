Therefore it is affirmed that we are either believers or unbelievers, separated. The believers into the eternal joy; the unbelievers into damnation. This is confirmed by the Lord, as John says in chapter … truly I say to you: whoever hears my word and believes the one who has sent me has eternal life and does not come into judgment; for he is thrust from death into life. Here Christ does not close from the youngest judgment: except that it shows that here, through the suffering of the unfaithful, the believers become wretched and fall into no division, wrath, or condemnation after this death, except becoming from death into life—yes, through death into life—and what they have hoped for/possess. From this we understand that the death of the believers is an entrance into life, and there is no return to the grave after this time; therefore we may help them. We have a clear sign/light in chapter XXXII. From the preacher at the Cross to Christ he spoke: “Now you will be with me in Paradise.” With this the preacher says: “Who will drive Christ away and die in the grave? Likewise other scriptures compel that belief and trust in Christ Jesus, the door of his suffering and death, no grave can reveal; then only condemnation is upon those who are in Christ Jesus.” Now only condemnation is upon them; so there is no pity, nor will there be punishment upon them, as in Romans chapter VIII.
