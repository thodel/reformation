Bread. And it then empties further, as such a setting and spying is arranged: namely, so that the spirit is given to the one, then the spirit is that which makes life. Thus they still see the person Christ living; they were greatly hindered by the recognition of the support of the spirit, as also the Lord says to them himself, by this Evangelist. cap. xviii.  

It is then that I go away, so the comfort of the holy spirit does not come. Now the spirit is that which makes life, the spirit that is sent by Christ, instead of making life here; it does not make it, it makes it understandable, as well as the effect of a spirit; thereafter the flesh is not useful, and there is still a Greek word οὐδέν which contains the negation; thus the world says it is completely useless, which then is the result after the exhaustion of the contrary material of which we now speak.  

It is far from us that we should consider the divine flesh worthy, otherwise it would be useless. But just as the Lord himself has spoken, we say that it is useless, then it is not ordained for that, and therefore it is assigned to the will of God, that He may not order the noble flesh to such a living food; therefore the thought here is of no use, contrary to the order of God.  

It is also of no use that already the wordly (Wyn) does not contain a Greek article there; that article has in its power that it strives for something further, as we also have in the beginning of the Gospel of John. In the beginning was the word καὶ ὁ λόγος ἦν πρὸς τὸν θεόν. So there is an article ὁ which does us still so much that we recognize, that indeed the word was by God, and how it stands with the name Spirit; Wyn is spirit; yet it is the spirit of Christ that makes life; so it is also not needed, that by the worldly flesh stands Wyn flesh.
