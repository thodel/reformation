It cannot be that they may be brought to an end: or that one of the elders of Bern has ruined the signs, made them bad, or sealed them; he has not struck or killed those of Bern, and it is not at all certain that he is guilty of having struck and ruined those of Bern.  

The last words of Paul: which, however, is useless and worthless, is useless and worthless, and itself a sort of punishment or condemnation, so that he does not decide the lychnam of the Lord.  

Having the sense that which is good for the night‑sign or thanksgiving and not rightly trusted in the Lord Jesus Christ, he judges that the night‑sign is used otherwise, not as a common meal.  

He judges the death of Christ to be nothing, which death, according to the literal lychnam, will be given as we previously sufficiently—see page 110 and 131, also shown: that when one speaks of the death of Christ, the prophets, evangelists, and apostles speak thereby through the literal lychnam.  

It also appears in the very words of Christ: “That is my lychnam, and that to memory of me: we shall not be forced to give thanks to the lychnam, except the death at the lychnam endured.”  

Thus he decides that here in the night‑sign, not with right belief, the lychnam is not the death of Christ; that is, he judges it to be nothing.
