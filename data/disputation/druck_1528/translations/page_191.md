The purpose of fasting, among other practices, as much as is in Scripture, I think it is sufficient for brevity, the saying of Paul in the first Epistle to the Corinthians, chapter vii, where he says: “We ought to demonstrate ourselves in all things as the servants of God, in much patience, in sorrow, in necessity, in anxiety, in persecution, in affliction, in watchfulness, in fasting, in chastity, in holiness, in long‑suffering.”  

Also in Joel, chapter ii, says the Lord God: “Be wailing unto me in the whole lamentation of your heart, in fasting, in weeping, in lamentation.”  

Concerning chastity we have it in the first Epistle to the Corinthians, chapter v, where Luther holds that half a chapter is about chastity.  

From these few, from many cited texts, it is good to note that the ordinances of the holy Christian community, the parts of which are narrated in the Epistle to Timothy, chapter iii, are those that some depart from the faith. They should be respected. The statutes of the church, therefore, the aforementioned works of practice, are well named in Scripture, whether they already belong to the holy Christian Church concerning the order, are rightly established, not to be despised, the obedience and the good, besides being regarded as good, for the use and promotion of the good, are imposed: then they know well that the righteous, according to Paul’s opinion, is not under the law.
